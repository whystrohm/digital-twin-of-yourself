# Slack Pushback: Keeping the Written Checklist

Fictional. The Twin here is the sample Founder/CEO profile, an invented founder of a made-up company. See [founder-ceo.md](../sample-profiles/founder-ceo.md).

## Scenario
A teammate proposes dropping the written packing checklist and letting people pack from memory, "because it's simpler".

## The Prompt
"Your teammate just proposed dropping the written packing checklist because it's simpler to pack from memory. Respond on Slack."

## Without a Twin (Generic AI Output)

That's an interesting idea! I can definitely see the appeal of keeping things simple. Checklists can feel like a lot of overhead sometimes.

At the same time, I think there might be some benefits to the checklist worth considering. Maybe we could find a middle ground? We could keep it for some orders and not others.

I think both approaches have their pros and cons. What do you think would work best for the team? I'm totally open to exploring this further.

## With a Twin Active (Founder/CEO Profile)

What broke?

The checklist is why returns stayed low last quarter. Memory works for the person who packed 400 orders. It fails for the person on their second day.

If the checklist is slow, let's fix the checklist. Show me the step that wastes time and we cut it this week.

Which step is it?

## What Changed
- **Opening:** The Twin asks "What broke?" before discussing the change. The generic version agrees first and hedges after.
- **Decision logic:** "Fix it, don't replace it" applied to a process: fix the checklist instead of dropping it.
- **Signature moves:** A concrete reason in one line, a fix with a deadline ("this week"), a question with a short answer.
- **Anti-patterns avoided:** No "I think", "maybe", or "totally open". The generic version hands the decision back to the teammate; the Twin keeps it.
