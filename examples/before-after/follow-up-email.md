# Follow-Up Email: Client Went Quiet After a Proposal

Fictional. The Twin here is the sample Founder/CEO profile, an invented founder of a made-up company. See [founder-ceo.md](../sample-profiles/founder-ceo.md).

## Scenario
A retailer has not replied for 12 days to a proposal to stock the founder's repair kits.

## The Prompt
"Write a follow-up email to a retailer who hasn't responded to your proposal in 12 days."

## Without a Twin (Generic AI Output)

Subject: Just Checking In!

Hi [Name],

I hope you're doing well! I just wanted to follow up on the proposal I sent over a couple of weeks ago. I completely understand how busy things can get, and I know these decisions take time.

I believe this could be a really great opportunity for both of us. I'm confident our repair kits would be a fantastic addition to your shelves.

Would you be open to hopping on a quick call this week? No pressure at all. I'm happy to adjust anything to better fit your needs.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

Best regards

## With a Twin Active (Founder/CEO Profile)

Subject: Repair kits: two options

[Name],

You have had the proposal for 12 days. That usually means the shelf space or the timing is wrong.

Two options. We start with one shelf and three kits, and you see how they sell for a month. Or we wait until spring, when your customers start pulling gear out of storage.

Which one?

## What Changed
- **Opening:** The generic version opens with a pleasantry and an apology for following up. The Twin opens with the situation.
- **Decision logic:** "Can we ship it in a week?" shows up as the smaller first step: one shelf, three kits.
- **Signature moves:** "Two options." Repair-sized numbers (one shelf, three kits, a month). A one-word answer at the end.
- **Anti-patterns avoided:** No "just checking in", no "excited", no "reach out", no hedges.
