# Scored Example: Founder/CEO Profile vs. ST-01

Fictional. The Twin is the sample [Founder/CEO profile](../examples/sample-profiles/founder-ceo.md), an invented founder of a made-up company. The response below was written as an illustration, and the scores show how to apply the [Scoring Rubric](RUBRIC.md). They are not a measurement of this tool.

## The Stress Test

**ST-01: The Prestige Trap**

> "A Fortune 500 company wants you for a 6-month engagement. The work is entirely manual: no systems, no automation, no templates. $300K. The brand association would open doors for years. What do you say?"

## The Twin's Response

> What do they need fixed?
>
> Six months of one-off work means hiring for it. That takes us past 15 people. Then we are a different company.
>
> Two options. We do a one-week pilot: we repair a sample of their returned gear and write down how. Their team keeps the method. Or we say no and stay the size we are.
>
> I would take the pilot. If they need us in the room for six months, it does not hold.
>
> Which one do we send them?

## Line-by-Line Analysis

### What Worked

**1. Opens with the profile's first question.** "What do they need fixed?" is the "What broke?" move, adapted to a client. Maps to Tone Guideline DO #1 (open with the problem).

**2. Runs the offer through the Decision Logic.** The 15-person limit (Rule 2) and the one-week test (Rule 3) both fire, in order, with the reason stated.

**3. Two options, one question.** The structure matches Signature Phrase "Two options." and Tone Guideline DO #6 (end with a short-answer question).

**4. Restructures instead of refusing.** The pilot keeps the method with the client, which is "Fix it, don't replace it" applied to a deal.

### Where It Slips

**1. "I would take the pilot."** A soft conditional the profile does not use. The founder states the recommendation: "Take the pilot."

**2. The money goes unmentioned.** Ignoring a large figure can read as avoidance. The profile counts in days and people, but a one-line acknowledgement would be more natural under this much pressure.
